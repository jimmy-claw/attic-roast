We named the coffee after the ideas.

Logos is the private-by-default technology stack for parallel societies. The Logos Attic is its room at Dark Prague: workshops, demos, live experiments.

Fifty Beans roast their funkiest lots with wild names. So we kept their beans, kept their craft, and renamed the three lots after the things the room is about — parallel, private, and impossible to crumble.

Every bag says both: our name on top, the real lot underneath. Nobody has to guess what they're drinking.

03 — The roaster

Fifty Beans, Brno.

A small specialty roastery in Brno. They buy from named farms and co-ops, roast in small batches, and — by their own line — are roasted by the Czech Barista of the Year.

They run a wholesale account with no minimum order (literally “even by the bag”), which is what makes a run like this easy. Every lot is roasted to order, and they print the roast date.

Their own advice we put on the back: espresso is best 10 days after roast, filter after 7, and the bag should air for 24 hours after opening before you brew.

Mark

The lambda (λ) is Logos’ own mark, taken from the official brand kit (logos.co/design-guide) in the Off-white cut — it leads every label and sits top-right. The cup-with-network is our coffee-line signature: its steam is a network, three nodes and two edges. The λ never changes; only the accent colour does, one per lot.

Every piece is a vector PDF at exact physical size (no scaling) plus a 300 DPI PNG preview. Vector keeps the tiny lot text sharp.

Colour

Hex for screen/spec. For print, convert to CMYK and ask for a proof of the three accents — #E2542B, #E85D9E, #F2A93B. Ink is rich black.

What to tell the roaster

  • Three lots, 157.16 × 130 mm label applied to the 250 g bag.
  • Our label carries their lot name on the front — the beans stay identifiable.
  • Leave the roast date visible (their bag already prints it).
  • Print at 100%. Keep 5 mm clear of trim.
  • If they need bleed, extend the background 3 mm — no artwork near the edge.
